The projects I find most interesting rarely fit into a single category. A research question becomes more useful when it can be translated into a working system. A technically elegant system becomes more valuable when it addresses a real need. And a promising product requires both a sound model of the problem and the discipline to test assumptions.

## One body of work, several perspectives

My background reflects this movement between fields. Business administration gave me a language for organizations, incentives and markets. Information systems connects that perspective with software. Mathematics added another way to reason precisely about structure and uncertainty.

Applied AI sits naturally at this intersection. My previous work ranges from forecasting demand at electric-vehicle charging stations to research software for physical experiments. Current projects include a knowledge system for municipal climate-policy information and experiments in building and marketing software products.

## Why write about the process?

A repository can show what was implemented, but it rarely explains why a particular approach was chosen, which assumptions failed or how the evidence changed the direction of a project. Those decisions are often the most transferable part of the work.

I will use this space for technical articles, research notes and project retrospectives. Some pieces will be narrow and practical; others will connect software to questions in economics, sustainability or society.

The aim is to make the reasoning visible—not only the final output—and to distinguish clearly between a demonstrated result, a current hypothesis and an open question.
