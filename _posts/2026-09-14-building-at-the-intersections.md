The projects I find most interesting rarely fit into a single category. A research question becomes useful when it can be translated into a working system. A technically elegant system becomes valuable when it addresses a real need. And a promising product needs both a sound model of the problem and the discipline to test assumptions.

My background reflects this movement between fields. Business administration gave me a language for organizations, incentives and markets. Information systems connects that perspective with software. Mathematics added another way to reason precisely about structure and uncertainty.

Applied AI sits naturally at this intersection. My previous work ranges from forecasting demand at electric-vehicle charging stations to research software for physical experiments. Current projects include a knowledge system for municipal climate-policy information and experiments in building and marketing software products.

I will use these notes to document that work: the choices behind a project, what failed, what changed my mind and what might be useful to others. The aim is to make the thinking visible, not only the final output.
